THREE WAYS WE GET ATTACHED

Why letting go
feels difficult.

01
Endowment Effect
/ɪnˈdaʊmənt ɪˈfɛkt/
稟賦效應

We may value something more simply because it belongs to us.

A cup used for ten years may feel worth more to you than the same cup in a store.
02
Sunk Cost Fallacy
/sʌŋk kɔst ˈfæləsi/
沉沒成本謬誤

We let money already spent affect our choices, even though we cannot get it back.

An expensive jacket stays in your closet, even if you never wear it.
03
Loss Aversion
/lɔs əˈvɝʒən/
損失趨避

Losing something can feel worse than gaining something of equal value.

You fear throwing away one useful object more than you enjoy the extra space.
THREE MORE IDEAS

Our things.
Our stories.

04
Extended Self
/ɪkˈstɛndɪd sɛlf/
延伸自我

Objects can become part of our identity and personal story.

An old award reminds you of who you used to be.
05
Just-in-Case Thinking
/ˌdʒʌst ɪn ˈkes ˈθɪŋkɪŋ/
萬一心態

We keep things because we imagine needing them in the future.

A spare blanket feels reassuring, even if no guest uses it.
06
Decision Fatigue
/dɪˈsɪʒən fəˈtiɡ/
決策疲勞

Making many choices in a row can feel mentally tiring.

Sorting an entire room at once can become overwhelming.
